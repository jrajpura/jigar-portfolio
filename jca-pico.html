How a JCA PICO gets built

Since January 2025, every new cancer medicine seeking EU marketing authorisation also goes through a Joint Clinical Assessment. Before the company writes a word of its dossier, member states say what they need to see: which Patients, compared with which Comparators, on which Outcomes. Their requests are merged into a set of PICOs, and that set decides how much evidence the company must produce and how much of it can come from its own trial.

The case: Drug Z in advanced Tumour X

Drug Z is a new oral medicine for adults with advanced Tumour X that has progressed after one prior therapy. Its pivotal trial, ZEN-1, randomised patients to Drug Z or Chemo A. About 40% of patients were marker M-positive; marker status was a stratification factor, but the trial was not powered for subgroups.

Two other published trials exist: B-301 (Immunotherapy B vs Chemo A, all patients, with marker M subgroups reported) and C-201 (Targeted drug C vs Chemo A, marker M-positive patients only). No trial includes best supportive care.

EMA days are active assessment days: list of questions at day 120, outstanding issues at day 180, opinion at day 210 (accelerated: 90, 120 and 150). The European Commission usually decides within 67 days of the opinion. The JCA deadlines come from Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1381; real procedures can differ, for example when an accelerated assessment reverts to the standard timetable.

What a JCA report does, and what it does not

It describes, it does not judge

The report sets out the relative effects of the new medicine against each PICO’s comparator and how certain those effects are. By law it contains no value judgement or conclusion on the medicine’s overall added clinical value.

Countries still decide

Member states must give the report due consideration in their own assessments, then draw their own conclusions and handle pricing, reimbursement and cost-effectiveness nationally. This is why each country’s comparator matters.

Gaps are reported, not filled

When there is no robust evidence for a PICO, the report says so. Comparators without a shared link to the trial often fall back on indirect comparisons, which Project 09 explains.
